# Scope Sheet

**Venture:** unnamed — bringing capable people from other fields into sectors that hire below their own need
**Founder:** Augusto — BBA, ESADE
**Last updated:** 6 October 2026

---

## The assumption this MVP tests

**A1 — Capable people would actually take these jobs.**

This is the one the whole venture now rests on. Two of three interviewees said the useful thing is finding people who are already trained and capable, rather than filtering the same pool better. But nobody has shown me that those capable people would say yes. The sectors pay less and carry less status, which is a plausible reason nobody looks their way in the first place.

If A1 is false, there is no supply to point anywhere and nothing else matters. So the MVP tests A1 and nothing else.

Not testing A2 (will the sector pay) or A3 (is the matching the hard part). Those come after, and only if A1 survives.

---

## What the MVP does

One sector, one page, one action.

A single page aimed at people working in one named adjacent field, showing three to five real jobs in one sector that currently hires below its need. Real roles, real pay stated plainly, no euphemisms. The page explains in a few lines why someone from that field would be good at these jobs, and the only thing it asks for is an email address if they want to hear more.

**One user:** someone currently working in the adjacent field I pick.
**One job:** decide whether this is worth hearing more about.
**One screen:** the page. There is no second screen.

**The beachhead I would start with:** equestrian in Catalonia, because the stable owner is the source of the insight and the sector is small enough that I can find five real openings by hand. The adjacent field is the variable I am picking by hand too — veterinary assistants, agricultural technicians, logistics, whoever the sector actually needs.

**What counts as proof.** Email addresses from people who actually work in that field, with a short note on why. The number does not have to be big. What matters is whether the people who sign up are the capable ones or the desperate ones, which I can tell by asking them in the follow-up. If the only people interested are people who would take any job, A1 is dead in the way that matters.

**What counts as failure.** Nobody signs up, or everyone who signs up is already in the sector, or they sign up and then say no to the pay once it is concrete.

## What it deliberately does NOT do

Named, so that I notice when I start building one of them anyway.

**No matching engine.** No skill taxonomy, no CV parsing, no scoring, no AI analysis of anything. I pick the adjacent field by hand and I am the algorithm. If matching turns out to be the hard part, that is a finding, not something to build around first.

**No accounts.** No sign-up, no login, no profiles, no saved searches.

**No payments.** Nobody is charged, neither side. Pricing is A2 and A2 is not being tested yet.

**No dashboard.** Not for me, not for employers. I count emails by looking at them.

**No employer side at all.** No company page, no job posting tool, no way for a stable or a construction firm to add a role. I find the five roles myself by calling people.

**No two-sided anything.** The moment there are two sides to balance, this becomes a marketplace, and marketplaces are what I wrote down as the thing to avoid.

**No CV upload, no application flow, no interview scheduling.** The handoff is an email from me, by hand.

**No multiple sectors.** One. Expanding to a second sector before the first works is how I would end up with two things that half work.

**No branding.** No name, no logo, no identity work. P1 in the architecture doc says no naming before validation, and I already broke that rule once.

## Rule for adding later

**I only add a feature when a real user does X, where X has to happen before the feature can exist.**

Concretely:

- I build a matching tool only when I have done the match by hand enough times that I am repeating myself, and someone has asked for it.
- I build an employer side only when an employer asks me, unprompted, to put their roles in.
- I add payments only when someone says they would have paid for what I already did for free.
- I add a second sector only when the first one has produced at least one person who actually took a job and stayed three months.
- I add anything automated only when the manual version is both working and annoying.

If I want to build something and cannot name the user action that triggered it, that is me building to avoid talking to people.
